## 1. Executive Summary & Context of Correction

Phase V0.8 established that:
1. True static freezing preserves reconstruction quality under attention coupling.
2. Sparse active-Q / frozen-KV execution with caching matches reference static outputs to 7 decimal places.
3. Sparse execution reduces recurrent block FLOPs by $16.78\%$ and delivers a $9.17\%$ latency saving over the baseline Fixed $K=16$ ($358.9 \text{ ms}$ vs $396.3 \text{ ms}$, speedup $1.125\times$).

However, **V0.8 contained three critical statistical and methodological shortcomings** in its headline economic claim:
1. **Hard-Coded Matched Policy**: V0.8 compared Sparse Oracle 5% against Fixed $i=15$. But under full-dataset evaluation, Fixed $i=14$ achieves an AbsRel of $0.009266$, which is *better* than Sparse Oracle 5% ($0.009274$). The correct, fastest uniform policy matching or beating Sparse Oracle quality is **Fixed $i=14$**, not Fixed $i=15$.
2. **Incomplete Benchmark Sampling**: V0.8 benchmarked only 7 of the 14 physical scans (`scans[::2]`).
3. **Unclustered Bootstrap**: V0.8 treated sequences independently during bootstrapping, ignoring that `subset_middle` and `subset_uniform` from the same DTU scan share identical 3D physical geometry and illumination.

**Phase V0.8.1 executes a rigorous, comprehensive correction**:
- Benchmarks **all 14 physical DTU scans $\times$ 2 subsets = 28 sequences** across Fixed $i \in \{12, 13, 14, 15, 16\}$ and Sparse Oracle 2% & 5% (1,960 timed runs).
- Clustered bootstrapping over the **14 physical scenes** (10,000 resamples), keeping both subsets together.
- Dynamically selects the fastest matching fixed policy in every resample.
- Strictly separates FLOP savings, latency savings vs $K=16$, and matched-quality headroom.

### The Decisive Finding:
When benchmarked across all 14 physical scans and evaluated against the true matched baseline (**Fixed $i=14$**):
- **Fixed $i=14$ achieves higher quality** ($\text{AbsRel} = 0.009266$) and runs in **$350.8 \text{ ms}$**.
- **Sparse Oracle 5% achieves lower quality** ($\text{AbsRel} = 0.009274$) and runs in **$358.9 \text{ ms}$**.
- The ideal, controller-free Sparse Oracle is **$8.07 \text{ ms}$ SLOWER** than the simple uniform stopping policy at matched quality (real speedup **$0.978\times$**; headroom **$-2.04\%$**).
- Across 10,000 scene-clustered bootstrap resamples, the mean latency headroom is **$-0.03\%$**, the 95% CI spans **$[-4.92\%, +4.68\%]$**, and **$49.77\%$ of bootstrap samples yield negative headroom**.

$$\mathbf{FINAL \ VERDICT: \ KILL \ Economic \ Claim \ for \ Wall-Clock \ Speedup}$$

Any trained controller would introduce additional inference latency and decision error, driving the net speedup further negative. The hypothesis that per-view adaptive halting provides wall-clock speedup on current GPUs over optimal uniform stopping is scientifically falsified.

## 2. Reconciled Technical Documentation

### 2.1 Distinction Between Regime C and Regime D
- **Regime C (Semantic True-Static Reference)**:
  A functional correctness baseline. Halted views have their hidden states restored prior to global attention, so active views attend to static representations. However, its unoptimized implementation executes full tensor allocations and redundant operations, resulting in latency ($404.4 \text{ ms}$) slower than $K=16$ ($396.3 \text{ ms}$).
- **Regime D (Sparse Active-Q / Frozen-KV Execution Engine)**:
  The actual compute-skipping implementation. Halted views skip frame attention and MLPs entirely. Frozen $K/V$ are computed once on the halt step and cached. In global attention, queries $Q$ and subsequent projections/MLPs are computed *only* for active tokens. Regime D matches Regime C outputs to single-bit bf16 precision (mean AbsRel difference $= 1.51 \times 10^{-7}$).

### 2.2 Reconciled Token Count & FLOP Formulation
At runtime resolution $H = 378, W = 504$ with patch size 14:
- Patch tokens per view: $(378 / 14) \times (504 / 14) = 27 \times 36 = 972$ tokens.
- Special tokens per view: 4 register tokens + 1 camera token = 5 tokens.
- **Exact tokens per view**: $P = 972 + 5 = \mathbf{977 \ tokens}$.
- Total sequence length in global attention ($S=6$ views): $N = 6 \times 977 = \mathbf{5,862 \ tokens}$.

Analytical recurrent-block FLOPs per view per step ($C=768$ channels):
- Frame block per view: $26 P C^2 + 2 P^2 C + 14 P C \approx 16.44 \text{ GFLOPs}$.
- Global block (active views $S_{\text{act}}$, total $S=6$): $S_{\text{act}} \times (26 P C^2 + 14 P C) + 2 (S_{\text{act}} P) (S P) C$.
- Full $K=16$ recurrent pass: **$3,642.0 \text{ GFLOPs}$**.

## 6. Root Cause: Why Hardware Realization Erases Theoretical Headroom

Why did the $+10.53\%$ step-equivalent headroom from V0.7 fail to translate into wall-clock speedup?
1. **Vectorized Dense Execution vs Dynamic Sparse Dispatch**:
   - Fixed $i=14$ executes 14 identical, contiguous, branchless PyTorch GEMMs across all 6 views with 100% GPU tensor core utilization.
   - Sparse Oracle requires dynamic masking, tensor slicing (`x[act_idx]`), dynamic KV-cache assembly (`torch.cat`), and calling PyTorch SDPA with uneven sequence lengths ($Q \in \mathbb{R}^{S_{\text{act}} P \times C}$ vs $K \in \mathbb{R}^{S P \times C}$).
2. **GPU Kernel Launch & Memory Bandwidth Overhead**:
   - The savings in arithmetic operations from dropping 1–2 views in late steps are completely offset by the overhead of irregular memory access and non-contiguous kernel launches on current CUDA runtime.
3. **The Controller Tax**:
   - This $-2.04\%$ deficit is measured on an **oracle** with zero controller compute. Any practical controller (even a 2-layer MLP probe) would incur additional forward pass time, further widening the gap in favor of Fixed $i=14$.
