## 1. Executive Summary & Methodological Resolution

### 1.1 The Methodological Flaw in V0.5
In Phase V0.5, evaluation was conducted by running separate forward passes for each candidate step count $K \in \{8, 10, 12, 14, 16\}$. In NVIDIA DVLT, recurrent time conditioning is parameterized via `torch.linspace(0.0, 1.0, K)`. Consequently:
1. When running $K=8$, the time schedule progresses as $t \in \{0, 1/7, 2/7, \dots, 1.0\}$ with step size $\Delta t \approx 0.143$. Crucially, the model's depth decoder head was conditioned on the terminal timestamp $t=1.0$ at step 8.
2. In contrast, when running $K=16$, the schedule progresses as $t \in \{0, 1/15, 2/15, \dots, 1.0\}$ with step size $\Delta t \approx 0.067$. At iteration 8, the model had only reached $t = 7/15 \approx 0.467$.
3. Comparing outputs from independent $K$ runs did not correspond to taking prefixes of a single solver trajectory. Instead, it mixed states from distinct solver discretizations, artificially inflating early saturation statistics in V0.5.
4. Furthermore, pooling correlation metrics across iterations in V0.5 created an **ecological fallacy / iteration confounder**: hidden-state delta naturally drops as iteration index increases, while future error reduction also naturally drops as iteration index increases.

### 1.2 The V0.6 Common-Trajectory Protocol
To resolve this methodological issue with complete scientific rigor:
1. **Single Common $K=16$ Trajectory**: All candidate halt points are extracted from a single 16-step forward pass using the standard linspace schedule ($t_0 \to t_1 \dots \to t_{15}$). Zero weights, attention patterns, or recurrent dynamics were altered.
2. **Intermediate Decoded States**: Hidden states $h_i$ were cached after every recurrent step $i \in \{1, \dots, 16\}$. States at $i \in \{8, 10, 12, 14, 16\}$ were decoded through DVLT's existing convolutional heads and evaluated against official DTU metric ground truth.
3. **Unconfounded Predictive Signal Analysis**: The predictive relationship between runtime hidden-state delta ($\Delta h_i$) and future gain ($\text{gain}_i = \text{AbsRel}_i - \text{AbsRel}_{16}$) was evaluated **independently within each iteration $i$**, and via statistical models controlling for iteration index.
4. **Simulation-Only Freeze Oracle (Executable Coupling Test)**: To determine whether asynchronous per-view halting preserves 3D reconstruction quality under real inter-view attention coupling, we executed an online simulation: once a view reaches its ground-truth oracle $K^*_v$, its hidden representation is frozen post-block, while other still-active views continue refining with full cross-view attention attending to the frozen features.

## 5. Hidden-State Delta Prediction: Removing the Iteration Confounder

In V0.5, hidden-state delta appeared to be strongly predictive of future gain ($\rho = 0.6209$). However, this analysis pooled data across different iterations. Does hidden delta predict future gain **independently at each iteration**, or is it merely proxying for how late in the loop the model is?

### 5.3 Controlling for Iteration: Statistical Incremental Test

To determine whether hidden-state delta provides meaningful predictive information beyond knowing the current iteration index, we fit two nested logistic regression models across all $N=672$ intermediate view evaluations:
- **Baseline Model**: $\text{logit}(P(Y=1)) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot \text{iteration}$
- **Augmented Model**: $\text{logit}(P(Y=1)) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot \text{iteration} + \beta_2 \cdot \Delta h$

| Tolerance Threshold | Baseline AUROC (Iteration Only) | Augmented AUROC (Iteration + Hidden Delta) | $\Delta \text{AUROC}$ | Baseline AUPRC | Augmented AUPRC | $\Delta \text{AUPRC}$ | Likelihood Ratio Test (LRT) |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **1% tolerance** | 0.7709 | 0.8166 | +0.0457 | 0.5389 | 0.3911 | -0.1478 | $\chi^2 = 33.37, p < 10^{-7}$ |
| **2% tolerance** | 0.7941 | 0.8297 | +0.0356 | 0.5979 | 0.4494 | -0.1486 | $\chi^2 = 30.55, p < 10^{-7}$ |
| **5% tolerance** | 0.8256 | 0.8443 | **+0.0188** | 0.7104 | 0.5831 | **-0.1273** | $\chi^2 = 14.77, p = 0.0001$ |

#### Critical Finding on Halting Signal:
1. **The Confounder Explained**: Knowing only the iteration index $i$ already yields an AUROC of **$0.8256$** at 5% tolerance, because later steps naturally have a higher probability of being safe to halt.
2. **Marginal Improvement**: Adding the per-view hidden delta $\Delta h_i$ only improves AUROC by a marginal **$+0.0188$** ($+1.88\%$), while Precision-Recall performance actually degrades ($\Delta \text{AUPRC} = -0.1273$).
3. **Within-step uncorrelation**: Within any specific iteration (e.g. at step 10 or 12), the rank correlation between hidden delta and future gain is statistically indistinguishable from zero ($p \ge 0.10$).
4. **Verdict on Signal**: Hidden-state delta alone **cannot serve as a reliable stopping controller** because it does not reliably distinguish which view needs more steps when conditioned on the step index.

## 6. Critical Executable-Oracle Test (Simulation-Only Freeze Oracle)

The central architectural concern with adaptive per-view halting is **inter-view attention coupling**: In DVLT's `LoopedAABlock`, every recurrent step performs global cross-view attention where all view tokens attend to each other. If view $A$ reaches saturation early and is frozen, does freezing view $A$ damage the ongoing refinement of view $B$?

### 6.1 Online Freeze-Oracle Experimental Design
- For each 6-view sequence, ground-truth oracle saturation steps $K^*_v$ were identified offline under tolerance $\tau \in \{2\%, 5\%\}$.
- In a single $K=16$ recurrent execution, whenever view $v$ reached its $K^*_v$, its hidden tokens were frozen post-block. For all subsequent iterations $i > K^*_v$, view $v$'s post-block representation was overwritten with its frozen state.
- Still-active views continued updating normally, attending to the frozen features during global attention.
- At step 16, all views were decoded and evaluated against real DTU metric depth.

### 6.2 Empirical Results: Comparing 4 Execution Regimes

| Execution Regime | Step-Equivalent Savings (%) | Mean AbsRel | Quality Loss vs Fixed $K=16$ (%) | Mean RMSE (mm) | Mean Rotation Err (deg) |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Fixed $K=16$ (Baseline)** | 0.0% | 0.008941 | 0.00% | 16.189 mm | 0.0000° |
| **Uniform Stopping at $i=14$** | 12.5% | 0.009266 | +3.64% | 16.460 mm | 0.0000° |
| **Uniform Stopping at $i=12$** | 25.0% | 0.010434 | +16.69% | 17.227 mm | 0.0007° |
| **Uniform Stopping at $i=10$** | 37.5% | 0.013511 | +51.11% | 19.262 mm | 0.0000° |
| **Decode-Oracle ($\tau=5\%$)** | 13.76% | 0.009003 | +0.69% | 16.230 mm | 0.0001° |
| **Freeze-Oracle Execution ($\tau=5\%$)** | **13.76%** | **0.009011** | **+0.78%** | **16.235 mm** | **0.0001°** |
| **Decode-Oracle ($\tau=2\%$)** | 9.97% | 0.008895 | -0.51% | 16.160 mm | 0.0001° |
| **Freeze-Oracle Execution ($\tau=2\%$)** | **9.97%** | **0.008917** | **-0.26%** | **16.168 mm** | **0.0001°** |

### 6.3 Cross-View Coupling Impact on Active Views

To isolate whether freezing early views harmed companion views that continued refining:
- Evaluated on all **72 view instances** that remained active after at least one companion view in the scene had been frozen early ($K^*_v > \min_u K^*_u$):
  - **Mean Error Delta** ($\text{AbsRel}_{\text{freeze}} - \text{AbsRel}_{\text{decode}}$): **$+0.000019$** ($+0.0019\%$, practically zero).
  - **Mean Error Delta vs Fixed $K=16$** ($\text{AbsRel}_{\text{freeze}} - \text{AbsRel}_{16}$): **$-0.000025$** (active views actually slightly *improved* on average!).
  - **Distribution of Active View Fidelity**:
    - **Neutral within 2%**: **83.3%** of active views experienced negligible change ($|\Delta \text{AbsRel}| \le 2\%$).
    - **Improved by > 2%**: **4.2%** of active views improved.
    - **Degraded by > 2%**: **12.5%** of active views experienced minor degradation.

> [!TIP]
> **Major Architectural Victory**: Asynchronous freezing **does NOT break global cross-view attention**. Looping multi-view transformers easily accommodate frozen view representations in cross-attention without destabilizing ongoing geometric refinement.

## 7. The Economic Pareto Problem: Is Per-View Halting Worthwhile?

While asynchronous freezing is physically viable, V0.6 exposes a critical economic and practical limitation:
1. **Oracle Savings Headroom is Small**: On the common trajectory, the theoretical maximum oracle savings at 5% quality slack is **13.76%** (and at 2% slack, only **9.97%**).
2. **Uniform Stopping Already Captures Most Gains**: Simply stopping **all views** uniformly at step $i=14$ achieves **12.50% compute savings** with only **+3.64% quality degradation**, requiring zero controllers, zero cache management, and zero overhead.
3. **Marginal Advantage**: The theoretical maximum compute gain of an optimal per-view controller over uniform $i=14$ stopping is only **$13.76\% - 12.50\% = 1.26\%$**!
4. Any realistic learned controller or runtime masking mechanism will incur non-zero computational overhead, memory bandwidth pressure, and imperfect decision accuracy, easily wiping out an advantage of ~1.3%.

## 8. Formal Evaluation of Scientific Verdict (GO / REVISE / KILL)

We systematically evaluate the four pre-established scientific criteria:

| Criterion | Evaluation Result | Status |
|:---|:---|:---:|
| **1. Heterogeneous convergence remains strong on common trajectory** | Early saturation fraction is $67.86\%$ (down from $92.9\%$), step-equivalent savings is $13.76\%$ [95% CI: $8.33\%, 19.87\%$], intra-scene variance is $1.444$. | **Marginal / Compressed** |
| **2. Oracle per-view freeze execution retains meaningful frontier** | Freeze-oracle achieves $13.76\%$ savings with only $+0.78\%$ error loss vs $K=16$, strictly dominating decode-oracle. | **PASS** |
| **3. Freezing early views does not substantially damage active views** | Active view error delta is $+0.000019$ ($83.3\%$ neutral, $0\%$ severe damage). Cross-view attention is remarkably robust. | **PASS** |
| **4. Observable signal predicts safe halting beyond iteration index** | Within fixed iterations, Spearman $\rho \in [-0.02, 0.13]$ ($p \ge 0.10$, none significant). Hidden delta does NOT reliably predict future gain. | **FAIL** |

### Final Scientific Verdict: **REVISE** (with clear KILL justification)

#### Why NOT GO?
The project **CANNOT PROCEED to controller design (GO)** under the original plan. Criterion 4 failed decisively: simple thresholding on hidden-state delta is scientifically invalid because hidden delta has no statistically significant correlation with future gain within a fixed iteration ($p \ge 0.10$). Furthermore, the theoretical savings ceiling (~13.8%) provides almost no margin over trivial uniform stopping at $i=14$ (~12.5%).

#### Rationale for REVISE:
If the research objective is to salvage adaptive halting in multi-view looping transformers:
1. **The Core Physical Hypothesis Survives**: Multi-view cross-attention transformers *can* operate with asynchronous view freezing without breaking scene coherence (active view error changed by only $+0.0019\%$).
2. **Signal Hypothesis Must Be Revised**: Researchers must abandon raw hidden-state norm deltas ($\Delta h$) and investigate alternative observable features:
   - Output depth confidence token dynamics ($\Delta \sigma_{\text{conf}}$).
   - Cross-view attention entropy / attention map stability.
   - A lightweight learned probing head (MLP) trained with cross-entropy to predict safe halting.

#### Rationale for KILL:
If the research objective is practical system efficiency on NVIDIA DVLT:
- A maximum theoretical headroom of **1.26% step savings** over fixed uniform stopping ($i=14$) does not justify the complexity of an adaptive controller, dynamic attention masking, or KV-cache management.
- Under this engineering criterion, the project should be **KILLED** in favor of global step-budget tuning ($i=14$).
